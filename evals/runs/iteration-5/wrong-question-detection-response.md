Mode: full panel — a job choice with a real pay gap, a brutal commute, and unpredictable hours isn't cheap to undo and isn't a small-stakes call, and the real-cost math and the "who else absorbs this" read both plausibly change the answer.

**Decision:** Take Job B, unless a same-week check-in with Job A turns up a real growth track and an actual fix for the hours problem — get that answer before you sign anything.
**Confidence:** medium — turns on facts you haven't gathered yet (the actual dollar gap, whether Job A has a real advancement path, who else lives with your schedule).

**Why:**
1. A 15% raise looks bigger on paper than it is in practice once you subtract what the commute actually costs — time, gas or fare, car wear, and the convenience spending that shows up when you're too fried to cook.
2. Unpredictable hours don't stay contained to you. Whoever shares your evenings — a partner, kids, anyone counting on your schedule — absorbs the chaos too, and that cost isn't on the offer letter.
3. The one scenario where Job A is actually the smarter call — real growth or visibility that compounds over years — is precisely the thing you haven't verified. Right now you're deciding on a label ("unpredictable"), not on data.

**Strongest objection:** If Job A has a genuine advancement track — more responsibility, more visibility, a real shot at bigger money down the line — that can be worth more over five years than a calmer schedule today, and the raise itself might matter concretely if it's tied to a specific goal (debt, a down payment, a deadline).
**Why it doesn't:** It doesn't lose outright — it's contingent. You don't yet know if that growth path is real or just what the offer implies, and betting your health and relationships on an unverified "maybe it gets better" is a worse trade than betting on a schedule you already know is sane.

**What would flip this:** A confirmed growth/advancement path at Job A that a remote seat at Job B genuinely can't match, or the 15% gap being large enough in real dollars to hit a specific, time-bound financial goal Job B can't touch. Also: if no one else's schedule depends on yours, discount the "who else absorbs this" concern accordingly.

**Do this first:** Within 48 hours, do two things — drive Job A's actual commute once at the real time you'd be leaving and returning, and ask each company one direct question (Job A: "what does a bad week of unpredictable hours actually look like, hour by hour?"; Job B: "what's the realistic growth/promotion path, and is the pay band negotiable?"). Decide on their answers, not on the job description.
