Worth pausing on that claim before treating it as a reason to switch: "people who stick with note-taking use Obsidian" is probably survivorship bias more than a causal effect of the app. Obsidian's user base skews toward people who are already deep into personal-knowledge-management as a hobby — they were going to build a sustained note habit regardless of which tool they picked, because tinkering with the system *is* the hobby for them. That doesn't mean the app made them consistent; it means consistent, tinkering-prone people self-selected into it. If your actual problem is "I don't stick with notes," a migration is unlikely to fix that on its own, and there's a real risk it becomes procrastination-by-tooling — reorganizing instead of writing.

So the first question is diagnostic: why do you fall off with Notion? A few common causes and whether switching actually addresses them:

- **Friction to capture a thought** (too many clicks, page felt too "formal" to jot into) — Obsidian can help here since files are just plain text and quick-capture plugins are fast, but Notion's quick-add / mobile widget can be tuned to do the same.
- **It feels bloated / slow** — legitimate Obsidian advantage; it's a local-first app, opens instantly, no spinner.
- **You don't reread or reuse old notes** — this is a linking/organization problem, not an app problem. Obsidian's backlinks and graph view make resurfacing easier, but Notion has backlinks and databases too; you may just not be using them.
- **Lock-in anxiety / want your notes as durable files** — real, structural difference: Obsidian stores plain Markdown files on disk, Notion is proprietary and cloud-hosted. If you care about longevity and offline access, this is the strongest actual reason to move, independent of habit-forming.
- **You want more structure (databases, relations, views)** — Notion is meaningfully stronger here. Obsidian can approximate it with plugins (Dataview, Bases) but it's DIY and can turn into its own time sink.

Practical suggestion: don't do a full migration as your first move. Try a one-week experiment — take your next set of notes in Obsidian on a subset of your notes (e.g., just this project) without importing your whole Notion backlog, and see if the habit actually changes. If it does, the file-ownership and speed benefits are a nice bonus reason to migrate fully. If it doesn't change your consistency, you've saved yourself an import/export headache and learned the problem is upstream of the tool — worth addressing directly (e.g., a fixed daily capture ritual, lowering the bar for what counts as a "note") rather than through another app switch.
