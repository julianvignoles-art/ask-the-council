Honestly, before picking a side — the "people who stick with note-taking use Obsidian" framing is worth poking at a little. That's likely survivorship bias: people who were already the type to tinker with plugins, folder structures, and Markdown files are more likely to end up on Obsidian *and* more likely to be the kind of person who sticks with note-taking in general. The app probably isn't causing the stickiness so much as correlating with it. Plenty of people build a durable daily-notes habit in Notion, and plenty of people install Obsidian, spend a weekend customizing their vault, and never open it again. So I wouldn't switch tools expecting that alone to fix a consistency problem — if the real issue is "I don't stick with note-taking," it's worth asking *why* the habit isn't sticking before assuming a migration solves it (too much friction to capture a note? no review habit? notes feel like they go nowhere?).

That said, if you've actually used both or have a specific gripe with Notion, there are real structural differences that matter:

**Notion** is a hosted, block-based database tool. Good for: structured/relational data (tables, linked databases, project trackers), collaborating with others, nice-looking pages, works great on mobile with low friction. Downside: it's slower (network round-trips), the block editor can feel heavy for fast freeform writing, and your data lives in their format/cloud — export is possible but not as clean.

**Obsidian** is local-first plain Markdown files plus a graph/linking layer, extended by community plugins. Good for: speed (it's just text files, opens instantly), longevity of your data (still readable in a text editor in 20 years, no lock-in), backlinks/graph view for connecting ideas, heavy customization. Downside: it's less turnkey — you have to decide on a system (folders vs. tags vs. linking, daily notes, plugins), sync between devices costs money or requires setup (Obsidian Sync, iCloud, Git, etc.), and it has basically no native support for structured data like Notion's databases.

A few questions that would actually settle it for you:
- Do you need tables/databases/relational views, or is it mostly free-text notes and journaling? (Notion and Obsidian-with-Dataview respectively)
- Do you share notes or collaborate with anyone? (Notion is much easier here)
- Does "owning my data as plain files forever" matter to you, or is that abstract? (Obsidian's real edge)
- Is your current problem speed/friction of capture, or something else entirely — like not having a habit/trigger for reviewing notes?
