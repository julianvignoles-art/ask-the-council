Before you spend a weekend migrating everything, it's worth pulling apart that claim a bit: "Obsidian is better for people who stick with note-taking" is likely survivorship bias more than a causal fact. The people you hear this from online are disproportionately folks who post about their note-taking systems — a self-selected group that skews toward tinkerers who *already* stuck with the habit and then optimized their tool. You don't hear from the much larger, silent group who tried Obsidian, got lost in linking/plugins/graph-view rabbit holes, and quietly stopped. The tool rarely creates the habit; the habit usually predates the tool choice.

So the more useful question isn't "which app do committed note-takers use" but "why am I not sticking with notes right now, and would switching apps actually fix that?" A few possibilities, and what they'd imply:

- **If the problem is friction to capture** (it takes too many taps/clicks to jot something down), switching to Obsidian probably won't help — Notion's mobile quick-capture is fine, and Obsidian's plain-text files can actually add friction if you're not already comfortable with folders/markdown.
- **If the problem is that Notion feels slow or over-structured** (you spend more time building databases than writing), that's a real Obsidian advantage — it's just files on disk, opens instantly, and doesn't nudge you toward elaborate schemas.
- **If the problem is you're not reviewing/reusing old notes**, that's what Obsidian's bidirectional links and graph view are actually good at — but only if you enjoy that kind of connecting-the-dots work. If you find backlinking tedious, it becomes another way to procrastinate on writing.
- **If the problem is just inconsistency/lack of routine**, no app fixes that — a change of scenery might give you two weeks of novelty-driven motivation, then you're back where you started, just in a new app.

For what it's worth, the actual feature differences:

**Notion** — great for structured/relational data (databases, tasks, wikis, team docs), good collaboration, everything lives in the cloud, but it's slower, requires internet for full functionality, and your data is locked into their format (export is clunky).

**Obsidian** — plain markdown files you own outright, fast, offline-first, extremely extensible via plugins, excellent for a personal knowledge base built over years — but it has a steeper setup curve, weaker built-in collaboration, and the infinite plugin/theme customization is a genuine time-sink for some people rather than a benefit.

If you do want to try it: don't do a full migration up front. Export a couple of weeks of new notes into Obsidian's default setup — no plugins, no elaborate PARA/Zettelkasten system — and just use it plainly for a month. If you notice you're actually writing more, migrate the rest. If the "let me customize my vault" itch shows up before the "let me write" itch does, that's a sign the tool isn't your bottleneck, and switching would just be moving the deck chairs.
